                                                  MEDIANA 2011

         The archaeological site Mediana covers the area of 80 ha and it is situated in the non-urban
Nis zone. The excavation in 2011 brought research of the villa with peristyle to an end. It is a
longitudinal structure that runs NE ­ SW and covers the area of about 6500 m2. It possesses a
central part in the shape of a peristyle and a monumental audience hall in the north, but also a large
number of rooms of administrative and residential character on both sides of the peristyle.

         The 2011 excavation was aimed at the northern, residential villa part, rooms around the
triclinium and the rooms in the south, i.e., at the villa entrance. Two trenches were opened in the
NE villa corner marked as 95 (7.30  2 m) and 96 (3.80  2 m). Two control trenches in the room
marked -22 contained layers of ancient rubble, lying of an older mortar floor. Beneath it there was
a hypocaust.

         In this area, running north, trench 97 was opened and to the north from it trenches 98, 103
and 104. In trench 97 (6.70  4 m), a rectangular structure made of boulders in a dry wall was
unearthed, leaning on the northern villa facade.

         In the control profile between the trenches 46 and 47 a marble head of a goddess was
discovered, most likely Venus or Diana. In the western room 22, the existence of two mortar floors
was confirmed. On the northern façade and within trench 72 remains of a destroyed furnace
(_praefurnium_) were discovered (Fig. 1), indicating that during the second villa phase, the
praefurnium and the hypocaust were no longer in use.

         Walls of the room 22 were decorated with frescos. On the floor of this room, a large
concentration of wattle with reed imprints was unearthed, originating from the ceiling and bearing
traces of frescos, which meant that the ceiling was painted, too.

         Trenches outside the NW villa corner include stibadium B and the area around it. The
entrance into stibadium B was positioned in the south. On the northern side, beneath the stibadium,
a destroyed hypocaust furnace was discovered. On the eastern and the western stibadium side,
rectangular annexes were unearthed with mosaic decorated floors and walls plated with marble. It is
certain that stibadium B was later added to the northern façade, while stibadium A was erected
simultaneously with the villa itself. During this campaign, the mosaic with floral ad geometrical
motifs was fully unearthed. A large number of mosaic tiles (_tesserae_) made of glass was found,
indicating that the ceiling was also decorated with mosaic.

         This year's excavations confirmed that during the second building horizon (from about 330
to 378) there were two building phases. During the second phase, the villa was decorated with
mosaics, frescos and sculptures. Although during the first phase, the structure mainly played an
administrative role, during the second one, it became a luxurious residence with ceremonial
character.
